Section 2 · The core of this guide

Teaching abroad: Agnes's realistic options

What a U.S. license does, and what it does not do

A state teaching license is the single most valuable credential for accredited international schools that teach an American, British or IB curriculum. These schools hire foreigners every year and handle the visa.

It does not make Agnes a qualified teacher in a country's own public school system. Teaching in Spanish, Italian, Greek, Portuguese, Thai, Mexican or Panamanian state schools as a full teacher requires that country's own credential recognition, the local language and usually citizenship or EU work rights. Government assistant programmes, such as Spain's Auxiliares de Conversación, are the exception, and they pay a stipend rather than a salary.

English-language teaching (language academies, tutoring centres, some bilingual schools) has different rules. A degree plus TEFL/TESOL/CELTA is usually enough, the pay is lower, and visa sponsorship is far less common.

Documents to start on now

  • Certified copies of the degree, transcripts and license, each with an apostille from the issuing state's Secretary of State. The UAE uses its own attestation chain instead.
  • An FBI Identity History Summary, apostilled by the U.S. Department of State, for Thailand, the UAE and most European visas.
  • A letter of experience from each employer on letterhead with dates and subjects.
  • Certified translations where required (Spanish for Mexico, Panama and Spain; Thai, Greek, Italian and Portuguese as requested).

Would extra qualifications help?

TEFL/TESOL (120 hrs) helps for bilingual and language-school jobs in Thailand, Mexico and Panama. CELTA is the stronger version and is sometimes required to teach English as a subject in the UAE. IB workshop certificates (Category 1) make a real difference at IB schools in every country. A master's degree raises pay grades in the UAE and opens university posts.

Should Agnes secure a job before moving? Yes, in every country on this list. Each requires the employer to start or support the work permit before she starts work. Moving on a tourist entry and job-hunting is not a legal way to start teaching, and repeated tourist entries are not a relocation strategy.

Teacher pay, benefits and contract quality

Figures are advertised ranges from job listings, recruiters and salary guides. None is verified compensation from a signed contract. U.S. dollar amounts use the rates on .

Representative offers: what might be left each month

Scenario only. Take-home uses a flat estimated rate for local income tax and social contributions. Expenses use the "comfortable" budget for that country, minus rent and insurance where the offer provides them, so nothing is counted twice.

How packages differ

  • UAE: housing or allowance, annual flight, medical insurance and an end-of-service gratuity are normal. Salary is paid 12 months a year.
  • Thailand: top schools add a housing allowance (THB 20,000–50,000), flights and insurance. Thai schools often pay 12 months but some pay only term time; check.
  • Mexico and Panama: overseas-hire packages at accredited schools may include housing help, flights and tuition for dependents. Local-hire contracts usually do not.
  • Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece: local contracts under collective agreements; social security covers healthcare; housing and flights are rarely paid.

Workload to ask about

Teaching periods per week, class size, prep periods, duty and supervision hours, after-school activities required, report-writing load, Saturday events and the school calendar (UAE schools run Monday–Friday; some Thai schools hold Saturday events).

Contract review checklist

  • Probation: length, and what notice either side gives during it.
  • Termination: notice periods, grounds, and what happens to your visa and housing if you leave or are dismissed.
  • Repayment clauses: do you repay flights, visa or recruitment costs if you leave early? Get the pro-rata formula in writing.
  • Renewal: deadline for intent-to-return letters and any raise on renewal.
  • Payment reliability: pay date, currency, whether salary is paid over 10 or 12 months, and any history of late pay (ask current teachers).
  • Immigration control: who holds your passport (it should always be you), who pays permits, and whether the school will release you to transfer.
  • Insurance: insurer name, coverage limits, outpatient and pre-existing conditions.
  • Housing: provided, shared or allowance; furnished or not; utilities included.
  • End of service: gratuity or pension contributions and when they vest.

Retirement, pension, Social Security and health benefits

State teacher pension: leaving a U.S. district usually stops service credit. Overseas teaching does not normally count toward a state pension unless the plan allows purchase of service. If Agnes is not yet vested, leaving could cost employer contributions.

Social Security: the Social Security Fairness Act (signed January 5, 2025) repealed the Windfall Elimination Provision and Government Pension Offset, which used to cut benefits for many teachers with a non-covered state pension. source

Totalization: the U.S. has Social Security agreements in force with Greece, Italy, Portugal and Spain, which can prevent double contributions and help qualify for benefits. There is none in force with Mexico (signed 2004, never ratified), Thailand, the UAE or Panama. SSA guide

Questions for her pension administrator and district:

  • Am I vested? What happens to my account if I resign now?
  • Can I take an unpaid leave of absence and keep my position or seniority?
  • Can I purchase service credit for years taught abroad later?
  • If I leave money in the plan, does it earn interest? Can I roll it over?
  • When does my district health insurance end? Is COBRA available and at what cost?
  • Does my state require continuing education to keep my license active while abroad?

Legitimate recruitment platforms

Schools and networks to research

Application plan

  1. International résumé (2 pages): license state and areas, curricula taught (Common Core, AP, any IB), grade levels, measurable results, technology, extracurriculars. Many Gulf and Asian schools expect a professional photo; European schools often do not.
  2. Cover letter per school: name the curriculum and one specific programme at that school; say why that city.
  3. References: three current supervisors, including the principal. Recruiters contact them confidentially.
  4. Portfolio: two lesson plans, an assessment example with data, a short teaching video, and a one-page teaching philosophy.
  5. Credentials folder: scanned license, degree, transcripts, background check, apostilles.
  6. Interview prep: expect a video interview, a demo lesson (live or recorded), safeguarding questions, and questions on differentiation for English-language learners.

Questions to ask every school

  • What is the full package in writing, and is salary paid 10 or 12 months?
  • Who pays for visa, attestation and flights, and is any of it repayable?
  • How many teachers left last year, and what is the average stay?
  • Can I speak to two current overseas-hire teachers?
  • Which accreditation does the school hold, and when is the next visit?

How to verify a school

Check accreditation directly with the accreditor (CIS, Middle States, Cognia, NEASC, WASC), IB authorisation on the IB's "find a school" page, and inspection ratings from KHDA (Dubai) or ADEK (Abu Dhabi). Read International Schools Review and ask in teacher groups for turnover and pay reliability.

Recruitment scams and bad fees

Red flags: a job offer with no interview; requests to pay for a visa, "processing" or a training course before an offer; pressure to enter on a tourist visa and "convert later"; personal email addresses instead of school domains; salaries far above the ranges here. Legitimate schools do not charge candidates placement fees. Search Associates charges a candidate membership, which is a disclosed service, not a placement fee.

Section 7

Visas, work permits and legal residence

Tourist entry is not permission to live or work. U.S. citizens can visit all eight countries visa-free or with an easy visa for short stays, but working on that entry is illegal everywhere here. Meeting a visa's requirements makes Agnes eligible to apply; approval is never guaranteed and consulates set their own document rules.

Is an employer permit tied to one school? In the UAE, Thailand and Panama, yes: the permit names your employer, and changing schools means the new school applies and the old permit is cancelled. In Mexico, changing employer requires updating INM. In Spain and Italy, the first permit is tied to the employer; later renewals become more flexible.

Section 8

Permanent residence and citizenship

Temporary residence is what a work or income visa gives. Permanent residence removes renewal tied to a job. Citizenship gives a passport. Being eligible to apply is not the same as approval, and processing can add years.

Portugal's change from 5 to 10 years took effect May 19, 2026 and is current law, not a proposal. Dual-citizenship notes need confirmation with a lawyer before Agnes relies on them.

Section 9

Renting or buying a home

Rent first (assumed preference)

Rent for at least the first school year. Contracts can end, neighbourhoods look different after a summer, and many offers include housing. Book 3–4 weeks of furnished temporary housing, view flats in person, and never send a deposit for a flat no one has seen.

Rent benchmarks (asking prices, not signed leases): Lisbon city centre €1,331, Valencia €1,217, Madrid €1,280, Porto and Málaga €1,050, Bangkok THB 25,000, Mexico City MX$22,000, Panama City $1,200, central Athens about $1,180. source source source

If she later wants to buy

  • Hire an independent lawyer who does not work for the seller or agent.
  • Verify title in the land registry and check for mortgages, liens and unpaid community fees.
  • Confirm building permits and occupancy licences, especially for renovated or coastal property.
  • Check the agent's licence where one exists (for example, RERA in Dubai).
  • Budget closing costs (often 8–15% in Southern Europe), annual property tax, insurance and maintenance.
  • Plan for inheritance: local forced-heirship rules may apply; get a local will.
  • Expect slower resale and currency risk when selling.
  • Financing for non-residents is limited; most foreigners buy in cash.

Section 10

Healthcare, safety and everyday life

Practical safety for a woman living alone

  • Choose a building with a doorman or secure entry for the first year.
  • Use ride-hailing apps after dark and share trips with a friend.
  • Enrol in the State Department's STEP programme and save the embassy number.
  • Learn the local emergency number and the words for "help" and "police".
  • Build a network early: school colleagues, women's expat groups, a language class, a gym or church community.
  • Understand local law, especially in the UAE, where public behaviour and online posts are regulated strictly.

Daily life checklist

  • Language: Spanish for Spain, Mexico and Panama is the single most useful investment. Thai and Greek take longer; English carries daily life in the UAE.
  • Banking: keep a U.S. account and card without foreign fees; open a local account once you have a residence card. Use a regulated transfer service.
  • Phones: an eSIM on arrival, then a local plan once you have ID.
  • Driving: an International Driving Permit covers short periods; residents usually need a local licence.
  • Bureaucracy: expect appointment waits (Portugal's AIMA, Spain's TIE, Italy's questura). Keep copies of everything.
  • Distance from family: Mexico and Panama are 3–5 hours from many U.S. cities; Thailand and the UAE are 14–24 hours.

Section 11

Taxes, banking and financial preparation

U.S. filing continues

As a U.S. citizen, Agnes must keep filing a U.S. return every year wherever she lives. Two main relief tools may apply, but she should not assume she qualifies until a preparer reviews her dates and income:

  • Foreign Earned Income Exclusion: up to $132,900 of foreign wages for 2026 if she meets the physical-presence (330 full days abroad in 12 months) or bona fide residence test. A foreign housing exclusion can apply above a $21,264 base. IRS 2026 figures
  • Foreign Tax Credit: often better in higher-tax countries such as Spain, Portugal or Italy, where local tax already exceeds U.S. tax.

Reporting: FBAR (FinCEN 114) if foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year; Form 8938 above higher thresholds. Her state may still tax her unless she properly ends state residency.

Local tax residency

Spending 183+ days a year in a country usually makes her tax resident there. The UAE has no personal income tax on salaries. Thailand taxes progressively (5–35%). The European countries tax salaries and take social contributions.

Online teaching

Teaching online for a U.S. company while living abroad needs: a visa that allows remote work (Spain DNV, Portugal D8, Greece DNV, Thailand DTV); the platform's own rules (some do not allow teachers outside the U.S.); a plan for payroll (U.S. W-2 or 1099 income, possible self-employment tax); and time zones (U.S. evening classes fall at 1–4 a.m. in Thailand).

Questions for a cross-border tax professional

  • Will FEIE or the foreign tax credit work better for my country and salary?
  • How do I end state tax residency in my current state?
  • How is my U.S. retirement account (403(b), 457, IRA) taxed locally?
  • Will a lump-sum pension withdrawal be taxed twice?
  • Do I owe self-employment tax on online tutoring income?
  • Does the end-of-service gratuity (UAE) count as earned income?

Section 12

Scouting trip and relocation timeline

13-day scouting trip: Mexico City and Bangkok

These two leading destinations sit on opposite sides of the world. If time is short, do Mexico City as a long weekend from the U.S. and spend the main trip in Bangkok. Ask schools in advance whether an in-person visit or interview is possible; many interview online only.

Mexico City · Days 1–5

  1. Day 1: Arrive; stay in Del Valle or Narvarte. Evening walk, test the Metrobús.
  2. Day 2: Morning commute test to the west side at school-run time; visit ASF area if a visit is arranged. Afternoon: two flat viewings.
  3. Day 3: Coffee with two teachers already working there (find them via Search Associates forums or school contacts). Visit a private hospital and ask about insurance billing.
  4. Day 4: Grocery shop at a supermarket and a market; price your usual basket. Visit San Pedro de los Pinos and Nápoles.
  5. Day 5: Spare day for a follow-up interview or viewing; fly out.

Bangkok · Days 6–13

  1. Day 6–7: Travel and arrive; stay in Ari near the BTS.
  2. Day 8: Morning BTS commute to Sukhumvit schools; afternoon in Phrom Phong.
  3. Day 9: School visits or interviews; evening meet-up with teachers.
  4. Day 10: Day trip to Nichada Thani (ISB area) and Bang Na to judge suburban life.
  5. Day 11: Visit a private hospital; get a condo agent to show 4 units.
  6. Day 12: Grocery and market prices; check air-quality app readings.
  7. Day 13: Rest, notes, fly home.

If something goes wrong

Visa delayed

Ask the school for a written later start date. Keep your U.S. job or sub work until the visa is issued; do not travel to "wait it out" on a tourist entry if you need to apply from home.

Offer withdrawn

Get it in writing and ask about reimbursed costs. Return to Search Associates and TES; late hiring continues through summer. Keep 2 backup schools warm.

Job loss abroad

Learn the grace period for your visa on day one. Keep the emergency reserve plus a flight home untouched.

Medical problem

Know which hospital takes your insurance. Keep U.S. coverage options in mind (Medicare later, marketplace plans on return).

Returning home

Keep your state license current, keep U.S. bank accounts and credit active, and save school reference letters each year.
